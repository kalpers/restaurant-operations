# Phase 33: Privacy, Loyalty and Gift Card Follow-ups Design and Implementation Plan

## Purpose

Owner request, 2026-11-19: fix five smaller gaps recorded as risks in the Phase 30, 31 and 32 completion reports, instead of starting a larger feature.

1. **Guest erase skips free text.** Erasing a guest (Phase 21) clears the profile and its visits, but a manager may have written the guest's name or phone number in the manager log or a pre-shift note (Phase 32). Today only a manual redaction removes it.
2. **Refunds keep loyalty points.** A refunded check keeps the points its visit earned (Phase 30) unless a manager adjusts them.
3. **Gift cards:** codes can only be generated by the app, so cards printed by a supplier can't be sold. Nothing lists balances left unused for years, which many states require to be reported as unclaimed property.
4. **Organization-wide figures in per-location reports.** "Outstanding loyalty points" (Phase 30) and "Outstanding gift card balances" (Phase 31) are organization-wide but appear in every location's report. Anyone adding the locations up counts them twice.
5. **Manager log categories are fixed** (Phase 32 D5), against the "prefer configurable tables" convention.

## Decisions for the owner

| ID | Question | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Scope? | **The five items above.** Out: event and private-dining bookings, FIFO lot costing, filing or paying unclaimed property, pre-registering supplier card stock in batches, points taken back for reasons other than refunds, cloud deployment. | The owner chose these five. Each item left out is a feature with its own rules. |
| D2 | Erase: automatic or chosen? | **Chosen.** Before confirming an erase, the dialog lists **possible mentions**: manager log entries and pre-shift notes whose text contains the guest's name, phone or email. The eraser ticks the ones to redact (all ticked by default), and those are redacted in the same transaction as the erase, with the reason "Guest erase GP-…". Unticked ones are left alone. | Text matching is unreliable ("Sam", "Rose", a phone number that is also an invoice number). Redaction can't be undone, so a person must confirm each one. Doing it in the erase transaction means it can't be forgotten afterwards. |
| D3 | What counts as a mention? | **Case-insensitive, whole-word matches of:** the profile's display name and the names on its visits (whole name, and any single name part of 4 or more letters); each email in full; each phone number's **last 7 digits**, ignoring spaces, dashes, dots and brackets in the text. Taken from the profile, profiles merged into it, and its visits, **read before anything is cleared**. Erasing a single unlinked visit (Phase 22) uses that visit's name, phone and email. | Full names miss "Mrs. Alvarez"; very short name parts flood the list. Phones are written many ways; 7 digits catch local numbers without matching every short number. Data has to be read before the erase clears it. |
| D4 | Which entries are searched, and who may redact them? | **All dates, at every location where the eraser holds both `manager-log.view` and `manager-log.redact`.** Mentions at other locations are not listed; the dialog says "N other locations weren't searched (no permission there)". The server recomputes the matches inside the erase transaction and refuses any ticked ID that isn't one. | Profiles are organization-wide but the log is per location. Redaction is limited to its holders (Phase 32 D11); erase must not become a way to redact anything without that permission. Recomputing stops a crafted request from redacting unrelated entries. |
| D5 | Pre-shift notes can't be redacted today. | **Add redaction to notes, like log entries:** `redacted_at`, `redacted_by_user_id`, `redaction_reason`; title becomes "Redacted", body emptied, note withdrawn. Once, by a `manager-log.redact` holder, audited (`restaurant.pre-shift-note-redacted`, without the text). Available from the erase dialog **and** as a "Redact" action on any note in Manage notes. | Past notes are read only, so without this an erase couldn't clear them. A standalone action also covers a note that mentions an employee's medical detail, which isn't about a guest at all. |
| D6 | Which module owns the erase now? | **A new workflow `src/workflows/erase-guest/`** orchestrates Reservations (find the guest's identifiers, erase) and Restaurant (find mentions, redact). Reservations and Restaurant each keep their own mutations. The existing erase routes call the workflow; their behavior without ticked mentions is unchanged. | It now crosses two domains in one transaction, which is what workflows are for. |
| D7 | How many points does a refund take back? | **The points per dollar on the refunded amount before tax, worked out on the visit's total:** points to take back = floor(eligible subtotal × rate) − floor(max(0, eligible subtotal − refunds so far) × rate), less what earlier refunds already took. Rate and subtotal come from the `EARNED` entry's snapshot, never from the current program. **Tips don't count. The per-visit points are never taken back, even for a full refund.** | Working on the running total means several small refunds take back exactly what one large refund would; rounding each refund separately would not. The guest did visit, so the visit points stay. |
| D8 | What if the points were already spent? | **Take back what is available (balance minus points reserved by a reward on an open check), down to zero, never below.** The entry records the points actually taken; the refund screen and the audit row say how many couldn't be taken back. No debt is carried. | The ledger forbids a negative balance (Phase 30). Taking reserved points would make a pending reward fail at close. A refund is a money fact and must never be refused because of points. |
| D9 | Refund before the session ends (no points earned yet)? | **Earning subtracts refunds already recorded on the party's checks from the eligible subtotal.** The `EARNED` entry's `eligible_subtotal` is then the net figure, and no reversal is needed later for those refunds. | Same result either way, with one ledger entry instead of two. |
| D10 | Refund voided? | **Put back exactly what that refund took** (new kind `REFUND_RESTORED`, positive). A refund that took nothing restores nothing. | Voiding a refund undoes it; the points should follow. |
| D11 | Ledger shape and which profile? | **Two new kinds, `REFUND_REVERSED` (negative) and `REFUND_RESTORED` (positive), each with a new `refund_id` column** (FK to `order_refunds`; unique per kind). Points go to or from the profile now holding the visit's points: the `EARNED` entry's profile, or the profile it was merged into (following the chain). **An erased profile, or a visit that earned nothing, gets no entry.** Runs whether or not the program is still on. Not audited separately: the refund's own audit row gains `loyaltyPointsTaken` and `loyaltyPointsShort`. | Ties each movement to the refund that caused it and makes it idempotent. After a merge the points live on the kept profile. |
| D12 | Who orchestrates refunds now? | **New workflows `src/workflows/record-refund/` and `void-refund/`** call the Settlements refund service and then the Reservations loyalty functions in one transaction. The refund routes call the workflows. No new permission: it is part of recording a refund (`settlements.refund` / `settlements.refund-void`). | Settlements must not write the Reservations ledger itself. Same pattern as `close-order` posting redemptions. |
| D13 | Supplier-printed gift cards: which codes are accepted? | **Letters and digits only, 8 to 24 characters after removing spaces and dashes, and not 13 or more digits in a row.** A code that is exactly 16 Crockford characters is stored like an app-generated one (same look-alike rules); any other code is stored upper-cased with no look-alike mapping. Lookup tries both forms. A code already on a card is refused. Only the SHA-256 and last four are stored, as today, plus a new `code_source` (`GENERATED` or `SUPPLIER`). **Please check your card stock: if its codes are 16 to 19 digits, see the alternative below.** | Fits Phase 31 storage unchanged. The 13-digit rule is the one the app applies everywhere (rule 15), so a payment card number typed into this field by mistake is refused. **Alternative:** accept all-digit codes too. The code is still never stored, but the app then can't tell a typed credit card number from a gift card. |
| D14 | Where is a supplier code entered? | **At sale and at code replacement, by the same people who can do those today** (`gift-cards.sell`; replacement `gift-cards.manage`), with a choice "Generate a code" (default) or "Use the code printed on the card". The supplier code is never shown again after the sale, like generated codes. | No new permission and no new screen. Pre-registering a batch of stock is left out (D1). |
| D15 | Report of unused balances? | **A new section "Unused gift card balances" in the Settlements area:** cards with value left whose last ledger activity is on or before the range end minus the program's **dormancy period**. It lists card number, date first sold, the location that sold it, balance, last activity date and months inactive, organization-wide, with no limit on rows. CSV as usual (no codes). The dormancy period is a new program setting, `dormancy_months` (12 to 120, **default 36**), changed by `gift-cards.manage`. Only ledger entries count as activity; a balance check doesn't. | Most states' dormancy periods are 3 to 5 years, so it has to be configurable. The app keeps no purchaser name or address, so the report supports a filing but can't be one; the screen says so. |
| D16 | Organization-wide figures in per-location reports? | **Keep them in each location's report, titled "(all locations)", with a note that they're not per location. In "All my locations", show them once** as a second section, "Organization-wide balances", below the by-location table: outstanding points under Sales, and outstanding gift card balance and card count under Settlements. They are never added into the total row. | A manager at one location still needs the figure. In the comparison it appears once, which is what the owner asked for. Gift card outstanding had the same problem, so it is fixed the same way. |
| D17 | Configurable manager log categories: shape? | **New `manager_log_categories`, per organization:** `code` (upper-case, immutable, unique per organization), `name` (1 to 40), `sort_order`, `active`, `version`, timestamps. The six current codes become rows for every organization, inserted by the migration. `manager_log_entries.category` stays a code and gets a foreign key to (organization, code) instead of the fixed list. | Entries don't change, so the append-only trigger and every past entry stay valid. Organization-wide matches how managers compare logs across locations. |
| D18 | Category rules and permission? | **Add, rename, reorder and deactivate; never delete.** A deactivated category stays on old entries and can't be picked for new ones; the last active one can't be deactivated. **New permission `manager-log.categories`** (Owner and General Manager), via upgrade seed `security.manager-log-category-permissions` (160 → 161). Not audited (as for notes); rows record who changed them. Managed on a new page `/manager-log/categories`, linked from the log. | Same rules as other configurable lists (positions, prep stations). A separate permission because Managers write the log but shouldn't reshape it. |
| D19 | Seeds, tests and leftovers? | **`db:seed` inserts the six default categories for any organization that has none** (idempotent; new organizations get them). The demo seed adds one supplier-coded card, one refunded check with points taken back, and a gift card old enough to show as unused. The validation fixture is unchanged. The Phase 22 manual SMTP/Twilio check stays with the owner. | Every organization needs categories before a manager can write an entry. Demo data shows each change. |

## Approved Phase 33 Policies

Approved by the owner on 2026-11-19: D1–D19 as recommended (D13 as recommended: codes with 13 or more digits in a row are refused). These are binding.

1. **Scope (D1).** The five follow-ups. Event bookings, FIFO costing, unclaimed-property filing, batch card stock, other point reversals and deployment stay out.
2. **Erase with confirmed mentions (D2).** The erase dialog lists possible mentions in manager log entries and pre-shift notes, all ticked by default; ticked ones are redacted in the erase transaction with the reason "Guest erase GP-…"; unticked ones are left alone.
3. **Matching (D3).** Case-insensitive whole-word matches of the profile's, merged profiles' and visits' names (whole, and parts of 4+ letters), full emails, and the last 7 digits of each phone ignoring spaces, dashes, dots and brackets; read before anything is cleared. A single-visit erase uses that visit's name, phone and email.
4. **Where and who (D4).** All dates, at locations where the eraser holds `manager-log.view` and `manager-log.redact`; other locations are counted, not listed. The server recomputes matches in the transaction and refuses a ticked ID that isn't one.
5. **Note redaction (D5).** Notes gain `redacted_at`, `redacted_by_user_id`, `redaction_reason`; title "Redacted", body emptied, note withdrawn; once, by a `manager-log.redact` holder; audited as `restaurant.pre-shift-note-redacted` without text; from the erase dialog and from Manage notes.
6. **Erase workflow (D6).** `src/workflows/erase-guest/` orchestrates Reservations and Restaurant in one transaction; the existing routes call it.
7. **Points taken back (D7).** floor(eligible × rate) − floor(max(0, eligible − refunds so far) × rate), less earlier reversals, from the `EARNED` snapshot; refund before tax; tips excluded; per-visit points never taken back.
8. **Shortfall (D8).** Take back at most balance minus points reserved by pending rewards, never below zero; the shortfall is reported, never carried; the refund is never refused for points.
9. **Earlier refunds (D9).** Earning subtracts refunds already recorded on the party's checks; `eligible_subtotal` is the net figure.
10. **Refund void (D10).** `REFUND_RESTORED` puts back exactly what that refund took.
11. **Ledger (D11).** Kinds `REFUND_REVERSED` (negative) and `REFUND_RESTORED` (positive) with `refund_id` (unique per kind); posted to the profile holding the visit's points (following merges); none for an erased profile or a visit that earned nothing; regardless of whether the program is on; the refund's audit row gains `loyaltyPointsTaken` and `loyaltyPointsShort`.
12. **Refund workflows (D12).** `src/workflows/record-refund/` and `void-refund/`; no new permission.
13. **Supplier codes (D13).** Letters and digits, 8 to 24 after removing spaces and dashes, never 13+ digits in a row; a 16-Crockford-character code takes the generated form, others are stored upper-cased without look-alike mapping; lookup tries both; duplicates refused; hash and last four only, plus `code_source` (`GENERATED`/`SUPPLIER`).
14. **Where entered (D14).** At sale (`gift-cards.sell`) and code replacement (`gift-cards.manage`), "Generate a code" by default or "Use the code printed on the card"; never shown again.
15. **Unused balances (D15).** Settlements-area section "Unused gift card balances": value left and last ledger activity on or before range end minus `dormancy_months` (12 to 120, default 36, set by `gift-cards.manage`); card number, first sold date, selling location, balance, last activity, months inactive; organization-wide, no row limit; CSV without codes.
16. **Organization-wide figures (D16).** Kept in per-location reports titled "(all locations)" with a note; shown once in "All my locations" as "Organization-wide balances" (outstanding points under Sales; outstanding gift card balance and cards under Settlements), never in the totals.
17. **Categories (D17).** `manager_log_categories` per organization (`code` immutable and unique per organization, `name` 1 to 40, `sort_order`, `active`, `version`, timestamps); the six codes inserted for every organization; entries keep the code with a foreign key to (organization, code).
18. **Category rules (D18).** Add, rename, reorder, deactivate, never delete; inactive ones stay on old entries and can't be picked; the last active can't be deactivated; permission `manager-log.categories` (Owner, GM) via seed `security.manager-log-category-permissions` (160 → 161); not audited; page `/manager-log/categories`.
19. **Seeds (D19).** `db:seed` inserts default categories for organizations without any; the demo seed adds a supplier-coded card, a refunded check with points taken back and an unused old balance; validation fixture unchanged; SMTP/Twilio check stays with the owner.

## Amendments decided at implementation

Approved by the owner on 2026-11-19 with the execution plan.

1. **One canonical code form (policy 13).** A typed code that is a valid 16-character generated-style code is read that way (O→0, I/L→1); anything else is read as a supplier code (upper-cased, spaces and dashes removed). Sale, lookup and reload use this one rule, so a code can never match two cards.
2. **Default categories (policies 17, 19).** Migration 0077 inserts them for existing organizations, seed `restaurant.manager-log-categories` for a fresh database, and the category service on first use for any organization still without them.
3. **Erase search scope (policy 4).** The erase routes read the user's full sign-in context and pass the workflow the locations where both `manager-log.view` and `manager-log.redact` are held. The location header check is unchanged.
4. **Refund split (policy 12).** Settlements exposes `recordRefundInTransaction` / `voidRefundInTransaction` with an optional hook whose result is added to the refund's audit row and response. The self-transacting `recordRefund` / `voidRefund` are removed; routes and tests call the workflows.
5. **Loyalty report (policy 11).** The by-date section gains a "Refunds (net)" column; refund entries carry the refund's location and business date.
6. **Note redaction route.** `POST /api/pre-shift-notes/{id}/redact` (`manager-log.redact`, current location).

## 10. Risks

- **Matching is a suggestion (D2–D3).** A mention written as a nickname or a misspelling won't be found, and redaction stays the manual fallback. A wrongly ticked entry is redacted for good.
- **Erase only searches where the eraser may redact (D4).** Mentions at other locations stay until someone with permission there redacts them.
- **Points may be short (D8).** A guest who spent points before a refund keeps that value; the shortfall is reported, not recovered.
- **Supplier codes may be weak (D13).** An 8-character or sequential supplier code is easier to guess than a generated one. Lookup needs staff sign-in, so guessing needs a staff account.
- **The unused balance report isn't a filing (D15).** It has no purchaser names or addresses, and the dormancy period is the owner's to set for each state.
- **Category foreign key on an append-only table (D17).** The migration must insert the categories before adding the key, or an upgrade over existing entries fails.
